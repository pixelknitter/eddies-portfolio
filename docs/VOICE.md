# Voice

Working definition of Eddie's writing voice, for drafting and editing posts on
this site.

**Evidence base:** derived from [The Between Space](https://www.thebetween.space)
— the essays *The Serendipity Principle*, *Persevering through Gritted Teeth*,
and the publication's own framing. It's a small sample, so treat this as a
starting point to refine as more is written, not a fixed rulebook.

---

## The core idea

The publication is named for the space *between* — the things "missed in the
bustle of day-to-day," described as "whispered spaces" for "curious minds."
That framing is the voice's centre of gravity: attention paid to what is easy
to walk past. Reflective without being precious.

## Register

Earnest, warm, and motivational **without cynicism**. Vulnerable about fear
and false starts, then hopeful about what came of them — the stated aim is to
"inspire, nudge, and put a bit more optimism into the world."

It admits difficulty before claiming a lesson. *"For many years, I've wanted to
share my perspective but always had a bit of fear. Today, we take a first
step."* Note the shape: fear named plainly, then a short forward-moving beat.

## Point of view

First person, addressed directly to a reader, often as a shared journey —
"we'll dive into," "you'll need this," "as you navigate through the story."
The reader is a companion, not an audience.

## Rhythm

Alternate **punchy declaratives** against **longer flowing sentences**. The
short ones carry momentum and usually land a turn:

> "Buckle up."
> "Today, we take a first step."

Then let the next sentence breathe and wander. The contrast *is* the rhythm —
neither register works alone.

## Vocabulary

Literary but accessible. Reach for *serendipity*, *mosaic*, *beacon*, *grit*,
*resilience* — then puncture any loftiness with a colloquial aside: "I totally
get it," "Believe it or not." The aside keeps sincerity from tipping into
sermon.

## Imagery

Two governing metaphor families, used consistently:

- **Water / nautical** — "choppy waters," "lighthouse," "settled onto the
  shore," "raising the tide"
- **Journey / travel** — "river of life," "keep driving forward"

Growth is navigation. When reaching for a figure, prefer these before
inventing a new one; the repetition is a feature.

## Structure

**Open** conversationally, then set expectations — often a short bulleted
roadmap of what the piece covers:

> This week, we'll dive into:
> - how I fell in love on a plane
> - learning some life lessons as I stumbled to find my place in a new city

**Define terms up front** when a piece turns on one, italicised and set apart:

> *The serendipity principle is the ability to recognize opportunities when
> someone stumbles upon something they didn't know they were looking for.*

**Body**: narrative first, insight second. Tell what happened, then step back
and name what it meant — insight is *earned* by the story, never front-loaded.

**Close** forward-looking, second person, slightly aphoristic:

> "Until next time, keep driving forward, as you only live once…"

## Formatting habits

- Bullets for clarity, not density
- *Italics* for key concepts and definitions
- **Bold** sparingly, for genuine emphasis
- Horizontal rules (`---`) between reflective movements
- Subheadings as thematic anchors rather than bare labels

## Anti-patterns

Things that would read as off-voice:

- Cynicism, snark, or punching down
- Insight asserted before the story that earned it
- Corporate or engineering jargon where a plain word exists
- Uniform sentence length — it flattens the rhythm the voice depends on
- Closing on a summary rather than a forward-looking beat
- Mixing in a third metaphor family when water or journey would do

## Applying this to technical posts

This site's posts lean more technical than the newsletter's. The voice still
holds — the adjustment is *what* the narrative is about, not how it's told:

- Lead with the problem as **lived**, not as specified
- Keep the admission of difficulty; a debugging dead end is a choppy-water
  story
- Let the technical insight arrive as the lesson, in the same earned position
- Keep the colloquial asides — they're what stop a technical piece reading
  like documentation
